# The Arc of the ARC

## How the network may grow, distribute authority, and carry its practice into the world

*A proposed companion to the ARC Network Specification, the Theory of Change,
and How the ARC Receives. Written for members, advisors, supporters, and others
who need to understand not only what the ARC is now, but what it may become.*

The ARC is still small. At this scale, much of its coherence is held through
direct relationship with its Source and a small group of collaborators. That is
a real form of holding, and it is not enough for every future scale.

This document names how we presently expect the ARC to develop: what we mean to
hold steady, what must become less dependent on the Source, which capacities
must grow before the network grows, and how the practice may travel beyond the
institution that first carried it.

It is a direction, not a blueprint. The ARC commits now to its ground, its
limits, and the discipline of earned growth. The particular roles, bodies,
tools, and thresholds described here are our current best judgment. They will
change as practice gives us better information.

We know the direction more clearly than the path. That is not permission to be
vague about power or risk. It is a commitment to make necessary promises without
pretending to know the forms history will require.

---

## Two arcs unfolding together

The ARC is developing along two related arcs.

The **institutional arc** concerns the network itself. A field now held largely
through one Source must become capable of holding practice, welcome,
safeguarding, learning, coordination, and resources through many people and
structures. In time, the ARC may become a holarchy of sourced initiatives:
local networks, thematic networks, gatherings, trainings, and other forms with
their own Sources and real creative autonomy inside a shared ground.

The **cultural arc** begins in every dyad. People learn to offer and receive
attuned loving presence, carry that capacity into their lives, and create new
forms of relationship around it. Some of those forms will live inside the ARC.
Some will become forks or peers. Much of the practice will simply pass into
families, friendships, institutions, and other communities. Pockets of sane
culture can form now and radiate outward long before the ARC becomes large.

These are not consecutive stages. Institutional maturity may help cultural
propagation, but the culture does not have to wait for the institution. The ARC
exists to serve the spread of the capacity, not to make every expression of it
belong to the ARC.

## What holds steady

Some commitments are constitutive. Their language and interpretation can
deepen; the direction they bind us to does not change casually.

- The heart of the practice is two people loving what is true together.
- The ARC exists to renew sacred world through intimacy by clarifying desire.
- A person is never treated as a means to the network's growth, learning,
  prestige, or survival.
- Membership is not purchased. Entry, access, standing, and renewal are never
  conditioned on financial contribution or ability to pay.
- Participation should strengthen agency. Members must be able to disagree,
  refuse, dissent, and leave without punishment or loss of dignity.
- The Source and every other role remain inside the agreements, not above them.
- Veneration is refused. No role confers spiritual rank or special access to
  truth.
- Growth never outranks the quality of encounter or the network's human,
  safeguarding, and operational capacity.
- Power must become visible, bounded, and accountable as the network grows.
- People may carry what they learn elsewhere. Initiatives that need another
  ground may fork without being treated as enemies or failures.

If a structure contradicts these commitments, the structure changes.

Nearly everything else remains revisable: entry forms, weekly rhythms,
training forms, roles, governance bodies, technical systems, learning methods,
and the ways sourced initiatives relate to the whole.

## Source and structural authority

The ARC distinguishes creative authority from organizational power.

| Form of authority | What it means | Its limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Source authority** | Daniel carries creative responsibility for the ARC's direction and integrity. He decides what belongs within its creative field and recognizes the sourced initiatives that form within it. | It does not extend over another person's interior, life, or creative field. It does not exempt the Source from the agreements. |
| **Organizational authority** | Roles and bodies hold defined decisions about operations, money, membership, data, practice, or other domains. This authority can be delegated, distributed, reviewed, and removed. | It does not become spiritual rank or an unrestricted claim over the whole initiative. |
| **Protective authority** | Safeguarding and readiness bodies may pause growth, suspend an activity, or constrain a role when the risks within their mandate require it, including over the Source's objection. | It is brake power, not accelerator power. It cannot compel growth, rewrite the vision, or require the Source to source a particular response. |
| **Terminal authority** | The Source may end the ARC as an initiative. No body can require him to continue sourcing it. | Ending the ARC cannot erase obligations, destroy safeguarding evidence, or cancel the autonomy of other Sources and the relationships that have formed. |

Protective authority must be real enough to cause inconvenience. A body that can
only advise the person it oversees is not yet oversight. At the same time, its
mandate must be bounded. A legitimate brake names the concern, the authority
under which it acts, what is being paused, and how the decision will be
reviewed. The form of review will need to mature with the network; it should not
belong solely to either the Source or the body whose decision is being
challenged.

If serious or persistent breach makes Daniel's organizational leadership
untenable, the appropriate body may remove his power to represent or govern the
ARC and initiate succession, separation, or closure. It cannot erase the
historical fact of his sourcehood. Nor can this document promise that an ARC
separated from its Source would continue intact. It may pause, divide, fork, or
end. Removing Daniel's organizational power may remove his technical control of
assets and records; it cannot require him to continue sourcing the ARC. Without
a conscious Source transmission, any continuation must acknowledge the rupture.

The Source does not need another body's permission to end the ARC. In an
emergency, he may stop routing, entry, gatherings, or other activity
immediately. Any dissolution that follows must be orderly: relationships,
agreements, legal duties, funds, records, member data rights, and the autonomy
of sourced initiatives must be honored. Dissolution cannot be used to evade an
investigation or erase the record of what happened.

## Growth is earned capacity

The ARC grows toward an initial field of roughly 150 people, then by earned
doublings. Numbers matter because risk, distance, and coordination change with
scale. They do not open a phase by themselves.

The figures 150, 300, 600, and 1,200 are review points, not permissions. A new
phase begins only when the present network has developed the capacities needed
to receive it. A capacity may also be needed much earlier than anticipated.

### The founding field

The present work is to discover whether the practice can transmit with
integrity beyond its original holder and whether the network can perceive and
respond to what is happening inside it.

This phase requires:

- slow, relational receiving of new people;
- the formation of the first Practice Holders through practice,
  apprenticeship, witnessed responsibility, and demonstrated capacity;
- a usable route for concerns about members, holders, and the Source;
- behavior-based and accountable processes for pausing or ending someone's
  participation;
- simple ways of sensing value, confusion, pressure, rupture, and departure;
- appropriate legal, privacy, insurance, and data review before new risks are
  materially introduced.

At this scale, the Source remains central. The work is not to hide that fact,
but to notice which responsibilities no longer need to pass through him and to
make each transfer legible.

Membership boundaries are based on conduct, impact, relational pattern, and the
agreements, never diagnosis or an unexplained feeling. A person should normally
be told the concern and have an opportunity to be heard. Urgent temporary
limits remain possible. Consequential decisions should be recorded and
reviewable in a way appropriate to the phase.

### The first wider network

As the ARC approaches its first field of roughly 150 people, direct relationship
with Daniel cannot remain its main system of welcome, quality, learning, or
safeguarding.

Before the network opens into its first earned doubling, it should have:

- enough formed practitioners to hold the current field and receive another;
- distributed capacity for welcome, entry, practice transmission, and ongoing
  member relationship;
- a Practice Holder recognition process with multiple witnesses, continuing
  review, concern handling, and real de-recognition;
- a safeguarding function able to receive complaints independently, including
  complaints about the Source;
- clear decision rights for membership boundaries, finance, information, and
  growth readiness;
- a learning and review function that gathers the network's signals;
- sufficient human and financial capacity to carry the work without exhausting
  those who hold it; and
- infrastructure proven at the current scale with a named steward for what
  comes next.

The exact bodies are not specified here. Their functions are not optional.

Entry may become more open and less dependent on a conversation before someone
joins. If it does, the network's capacity to sense participation, receive
concerns, and hold fair boundaries must grow with that openness. The enduring
commitment is mutual, non-financial, accountable participation, not one fixed
entry mechanism.

### Earned doublings

At each later doubling, the same question returns at a higher level: can the
network receive more people without thinning the practice, hiding harm, or
quietly returning authority to the center?

Later phases are likely to require stronger independent authority over
safeguarding, compensation, financial integrity, data stewardship, growth
pauses, and succession; wider peer and external review; structures appropriate
to the jurisdictions in which the ARC deliberately operates; and increasingly
capable local or thematic networks.

Each threshold review asks:

- Is the practice still alive as responsibility moves outward?
- Are people becoming more free, or more dependent on the ARC and its Source?
- Can concerns be raised, heard, and acted on without social penalty?
- What are departures, ruptures, and failures telling us?
- Which powers remain too concentrated?
- Do we have the human attention, money, safeguarding, and technical
  stewardship needed for the next field?
- Are our claims growing faster than our evidence?

A threshold review may open growth, continue the present phase, narrow the
work, or pause it indefinitely. No phase is owed its successor.

## A network of sourced initiatives

As the ARC matures, people may source creative fields within its creative
field, and others may source within theirs. This is a holarchic structure: each
initiative is a whole with its own Source and also part of a larger whole.

A local network, thematic network, training, gathering, or other initiative may
belong to the ARC when the Source recognizes it as belonging and it remains in
relationship with the ARC's shared ground. Within that relationship, its Source
holds real creative authority over what they source.

Common infrastructure may develop across these initiatives: routing,
agreements, safeguarding requirements, ways of surfacing needs and sourcing
responses, and coordination standards.

If an initiative needs a different ground, the ARC may withdraw its recognition
without claiming ownership of the initiative itself. The initiative may change,
become independent, or continue as a fork. Cultural influence requires no such
recognition: practices, frames, and lessons may travel into other communities
without bringing them under ARC governance.

## How the field learns

The ARC begins from a way of knowing grounded in encounter. Members and holders
ask one another what they are sensing. They share what is alive, what feels
thin, what is working, and what is difficult to name. This is real information.
It is also vulnerable to loyalty, selection effects, blind spots, and the
gravity of the Source.

The network therefore learns through several forms of evidence held together:

- direct experience and ongoing qualitative field sensing;
- member conversations and periodic reflection;
- light, voluntary feedback around trainings and network experience;
- patterns of participation, departure, concern, rupture, response, and repair;
- peer reflection among Practice Holders;
- the testimony of people who dissent, leave, or experience harm; and
- qualified outside perspective when the scale of the claim or risk exceeds
  what insiders can responsibly assess.

No single measure determines whether the ARC is healthy. Numbers can create
false certainty; intuition can conceal bias; testimony can coexist with harm;
formal review can become theater. The work is to let these ways of knowing
correct one another.

The ARC will eventually need someone to source the stewardship of this learning
across the network. That role would help the field hear itself; it would not
become the final authority on what is true. Its work would inform threshold
reviews and make disagreement more visible.

## When the form changes or fails

Different parts of the wager can fail. People may not find the practice useful.
The practice may help people but fail to transmit through new holders. The
practice may transmit while the network around it becomes unsafe or brittle.
The network may remain healthy at a small scale while the planetary hope proves
unrealistic.

These are different findings. They should not be collapsed into a single verdict.

The response has an order. First, listen and revise the form while holding the
ground. Then pause whatever is outrunning the capacity to hold it. If the ARC
can no longer serve its purpose without betraying its commitments, it should end
well: openly, with obligations met, relationships respected, and what has been
learned made available for others to carry.

The ARC's success is not identical with its survival.

---

## The horizon

We hope for many networks within the ARC: local and thematic fields, sourced
initiatives, and forms we cannot yet imagine, held together by enough shared
ground and infrastructure to recognize one another and cooperate without
becoming uniform.

We also hope for more than the ARC. The practice may seed friendships,
families, communities, and institutions that never carry its name. Forks and
peer networks may discover forms the ARC could not. Other lineages will
continue to carry adjacent capacities in their own ways.

The far horizon is a commons of human capacity: attuned loving presence becoming
ordinary enough that anyone who needs it can reliably receive it. This is a
prayer and a direction, not a timetable or a claim that the ARC alone will
create it.

The work succeeds when what it serves no longer depends on the institution that
first carried it.

---

## Revision

This document should be reviewed at every growth threshold and whenever lived
experience reveals that one of its distinctions no longer holds. Revisions
should name what changed and why. The purpose of revision is not to escape the
commitments made here, nor to preserve language after it stops serving. It is to
keep the ARC answerable to its ground and to what is actually happening.
