# How the ARC Receives

The full statement of the ARC's resourcing philosophy, for those considering supporting the work.

*This document is the companion to Section 10 of the ARC Network Specification, which describes the network this philosophy serves. The specification comes first.*

---

## How the work is supported

The ARC is supported in exactly two ways: revenue from Foundations and other training, and gifts from people who recognize the work's value. There is no third stream.

How money moves through the ARC is itself part of the design. A network organized around intimate encounter cannot receive its resources in ways that quietly instrumentalize the people inside it. The resourcing model must embody the network's commitments, not contradict them. What follows is how we hold that.

---

## Membership is never purchased

Membership is not purchased. The practice and relational life of the network are freely available to members. Entry, access, standing, and renewal are never conditioned on financial contribution.

Members and others who wish to help resource the work may give voluntarily. No contribution is required or expected, and giving has no bearing on membership, access, standing, relationships, or voice in the network. A member who gives nothing and a member who gives substantially stand in the same relationship to the ARC.

Membership generates no revenue, and it never will. The moment belonging can be bought, the practice at the center of the network is compromised. This line is structural, not aspirational, and it is the first thing anyone considering supporting the work should understand.

---

## Why "gifts" rather than "investments"

The ARC will not be commercially valuable in the way investments are. We do not intend to create proprietary products, capture markets, or generate returns on capital.

The ARC is a public-benefit initiative, being built in service of an intimate planetary culture. Like a national park, a great public library, or a work of art sustained through patronage, its value is meant to reach far beyond those who fund it. At this early stage, a relatively small number of substantial gifts could profoundly change what becomes possible.

We use the language of gift deliberately. A gift creates relationship without creating obligation. It honors the agency of both giver and receiver. It carries no claim — no ownership, no control, no say over the direction of the work beyond the relationship itself. It is the resource form that matches the work.

This is consistent with frameworks like Source Principles (Peter Koenig, Tom Nixon), which distinguish gift-based resourcing from investment-based resourcing as fundamentally different acts. We recognize the patron, not the investor.

---

## Why "relationship" rather than "zero strings"

There is a model, increasingly common in serious wisdom-tradition work and in the patronage of independent teachers and creators, that holds resources should arrive without strings of any kind. No reporting, no accountability, no expectation of relationship. The principle is that strings are how good projects become captured.

The ARC's stance is adjacent but distinct.

Strings — meaning conditions, expectations, control levers — are indeed how projects become captured, and we reject them. But the answer is not the elimination of relationship. It is the *deepening* of relationship.

The ARC's relationship with its patrons is not transactional. It is also not arms-length. It is relational: a real connection between people who are doing something together, in which honesty, discernment, and mutual care are present. Reporting, in this frame, is not accountability imposed from outside; it is a basic expression of being in real relationship with another person about something that matters.

It would be incoherent for a project organized around the deepening of human relationship to receive its resources through depersonalized channels.

---

## What this looks like in practice

**Reporting.** We will report honestly to those who give, on what their gift enabled. This is not an accountability requirement imposed on the ARC. It is a basic expression of relationship. We do not believe transparency is the enemy of integrity; we believe opacity is.

**Discernment, in both directions.** We are selective about who participates as patron, just as we are selective about who participates as practitioner. Not everyone with capital is a good fit. Not every kind of help serves the work. We will decline gifts whose conditions, framing, or origin would risk compromising the project. Equally, we expect those considering supporting us to do their own discernment work — to look closely, ask hard questions, and walk away if the fit isn't right.

**Trust architecture.** The structural commitments that hold the receiving of resources clean are the same ones that safeguard the network as a whole:

- *The Source Agreement*, with its visible safeguarding commitments — a binding covenant, not a statement of intent.
- *The Member Agreement*, which holds for the Source as it holds for every member.
- *The income cap.* Daniel will not take more than $250,000 per year in personal income from the ARC — a personal ceiling that does not move with the network's revenue. The ARC is not a vehicle for personal enrichment.
- *An explicit right of exit*, for every member, always.
- *Visible financial flows.* What the ARC receives, what it spends, and what it pays its collaborators will be visible to those participating in the project at the appropriate level of relationship.
- *The refusal of veneration.* The Source is a practitioner-leader, not a guru, and is committed in writing to redirecting idealization when it arrives.

The fuller anti-cult design document is available on request.

**Honest acknowledgment of failure.** The ARC will harm and fail in some respects. We say this directly to anyone considering supporting us, because integrity here begins with the recognition that no real project survives contact with reality without real mistakes. What we offer in return is not the promise of perfection but the structures and relationships that allow repair.

## What we offer

- Honest reporting on what your gift enabled
- Ongoing relationship to the work as it unfolds
- For those who wish, deeper relationship with the network itself
- Honesty about what's hard, what's failing, and what's working

---

## What we do not offer

- Equity, returns, or any financial upside
- Influence over the project's direction beyond your relationship with us
- Standing in the network — giving has no bearing on membership, access, or voice
- Legitimacy through association — we will not list patrons publicly without explicit permission, and we will not use patron names to attract other patrons
- Speed. We move at the pace the work requires. People who need quick optics or fast wins are not a fit for the ARC

---

## On the kinds of conversations that work

The ARC's patron relationships are built through real conversation, over time, with people who feel the longing the work responds to and who have the capacity to help.

What works:
- A long conversation in person or via video
- A document like this one or the Network Specification, read carefully, with questions that follow
- The honest articulation of what someone is hoping for and what they would be unable to live with
- The willingness to walk away if the fit isn't right, on either side

What doesn't work:
- Pitches in compressed time
- Cold outreach
- Conversations in which the patron is treated as a wallet rather than as a person
- Conversations in which the project is being sold

## The order of the work

The ARC's three current resourcing aims, in order:

1. **Liberate the founding collaborators.** John first, then Daniel. The work is currently carried by people giving more than they are paid for. That is right for a founding period and wrong as a permanent condition: a project about the end of extraction cannot run on the quiet depletion of the people who hold it. The aim is modest sustainability, not enrichment — for the Source, it is bounded from above by the income cap named here.
2. **Build sustainable training revenue.** Foundations, the ARC's eight-week training in dyadic unfolding, is the network's internal source of revenue, and other trainings may join it in time. Membership generates nothing, by design. Over time, training revenue should carry more and more of the operational weight, so that gifts fund what becomes possible rather than what keeps the lights on.
3. **Develop the infrastructure that allows scaling.** Practice Holders, training pathways, technical and network infrastructure. This is where longer-term patron support is most needed.

These are nested. Resources that arrive in Phase 1 do not stay in Phase 1; the project moves through them.

## Where we are now

The ARC is open. The first public phase is underway. The Weekly Weave is live, routing members into regular dyadic practice. Foundations continues to be offered and to evolve. People are entering the network through the Integration Pathway.

The ARC has received its first funding, and further conversations are underway with people who feel called to help this work flourish. Our needs in this phase are concrete and small relative to what the project will eventually require.

We are not running a fundraising campaign. We are building real relationships with people who feel the longing this work is responding to, and who have the capacity to help.

If you are reading this and feel something stir, we would be glad to speak. The network's public face is at [arcnetwork.is](https://arcnetwork.is).
