# The ARC Entryway
## Entering the ARC — Design Specification

> A free relational container for orientation, relationship, and mutual discernment. Texts, a shared space, conversations as they are needed, and a door held by the entry conversation.

---

## 1. What this is

The Entryway is the one way into the ARC. It brings a person into sustained contact with the practice, people, commitments, and culture of the network, and it ends in a mutual choice. It is a relational process, not a signup or transaction (Network Spec v1, §5).

This document supersedes the Integration Pathway specification (June 2026) and the two-track entry architecture that preceded it. What changed, and why:

- **The name.** "Integration Pathway" described a door for practitioners of adjacent lineages. The Entryway is for everyone who enters — whether their grounding came through the ARC's own Foundations Training or through another body of relational, somatic, or contemplative practice. *Entering the ARC* and *the ARC Entryway* are both sanctioned frames, used by context.
- **It is free.** The monthly contribution is retired (D22, D27). Membership is not purchased, and neither is the approach to it. Nothing about a person's time in the Entryway carries a financial dimension.
- **One door, not two.** Foundations is an offering in its own right — a training, not an entry track. Some Foundations participants may later feel drawn toward the ARC; if so, they enter the way everyone enters: through the Entryway and its entry conversation.
- **The discernment process lives here.** What was sketched for a separate Discernment Process document is Section 5 of this specification (D34).

What has not changed: the entry conversation remains the door, and the discernment is mutual. The Entryway delivers people to that conversation; it does not replace it.

## 2. Who it is for

People seriously considering membership who already have sufficient grounding in dyadic unfolding or a closely related practice. That grounding may have come through Foundations or through another body of relational, somatic, or contemplative practice — Aletheia Unfolding, Hakomi, IFS, Focusing-lineage work, and kindred formations. A working list of trainings functions as a heuristic, not a gate: recognized training grants access to the entry conversation, never entry itself.

The slow lane remains real and reachable. Some people clearly carry the practice without a formal credential — formed through long meditation, contemplative therapy, years of relational work that never became a certificate. They may request a longer entry conversation, possibly including a sample dyadic encounter. The list stays humble; the door stays open.

What arrivals need here is not formation in the practice but orientation — to the ARC's language, its commitments, its culture, and the particular emphases of its form.

## 3. The shape of the Entryway

Three elements, then a door.

**The texts.** A small versioned packet: the Entryway's own overview document; Crossing Over (for those arriving from adjacent lineages); the Logos Text doorway pages (Welcome, The Longing, Our Fundamental Commitments) with a curated set of starting nodes; and the Member Agreement, read early so the choice at the end is informed. The live public packet at arcnetwork.is/discernment is the source of truth for what circulates (D24); this packet points there rather than duplicating it.

**The shared space.** A single ongoing WhatsApp space for everyone currently in the Entryway, where questions can be asked and the culture felt from its edge. Held, not moderated at arm's length; Daniel is present in it. What is shared there stays there. (The June Telegram design is retired; the Entryway lives on WhatsApp.)

**The conversations.** Group conversations called when there is enough aliveness in the group's questions to warrant gathering, and one-on-ones available once someone has genuinely encountered the material. These are orientation conversations, not the entry conversation; they meet what the texts and the field bring up.

There is no fixed duration and no timetable. A person may step away whenever continuing no longer feels right, and leaving is one message — no exit process, no friction. Participation creates no expectation of membership. The Entryway is open-ended by design; how people actually use it will teach us whether it needs more structure (D27 — no vestibule mechanism, deliberately; we iterate from reality).

## 4. The door: the entry conversation

When a person feels ready — on their own timing — they request the **entry conversation**. It is the central moment of the Entryway, and it does four things, weighted differently in different cases:

- **Confirms what is claimed** — whether the practice named has actually been inhabited.
- **Senses the practice the person carries** — whether it can meet the ARC's particular articulation.
- **Introduces the language in dialogue** — how terms like presence, attunement, and ways of looking land is itself diagnostic.
- **Holds mutual discernment about fit** — not only *is this person ready for the ARC* but *is the ARC right for them, now*.

The conversation holds the spec's three questions (v1 §5):

> Does this person want to enter the commitments and practice of the ARC?
>
> Is the ARC able and willing to receive them well?
>
> Is this the right relationship for both, now?

Entry requires a mutual yes. A no or not-now, from either side, is a complete outcome of the process — sometimes the door opens later; sometimes the door is genuinely elsewhere. Time spent in the Entryway has no bearing on the outcome, and the outcome is communicated in conversation, not by email or form.

On a mutual yes, the person signs the Member Agreement and joins the ARC: the Weekly Weave, the Gathering, the broader field.

## 5. Mutual discernment

Discernment is not a gate placed at the end of the Entryway. It is the process the Entryway exists to support, unfolding through understanding, relationship, and conversation until it becomes clear enough to culminate.

The asymmetry the old design carried — the network validating the arriving person — is gone from the frame. The second question is the ARC's own examination: whether *we* are able and willing to receive this person well. Both sides are discerning; both sides can say not-now.

## 6. Holding and diffusion

Daniel holds the Entryway in this phase, with John Ooi co-holding much of the entry conversation work. In time this distributes — Practice Holders carrying orientation conversations and eventually entry conversations, with the Source participating in the ARC's side of discernment rather than monopolizing it. Nothing about the Entryway should structurally require Daniel beyond the founding phase.

## 7. Safeguarding

The money-conditioned safeguards of the June design retire with the money. What survives, stated plainly in the Entryway's overview document:

- Participation never purchases or guarantees passage. The entry conversation is free to arrive at any of its outcomes.
- Exit is frictionless and honored. Leaving is a fine outcome, said so out loud.
- No one is moved toward the door on a timetable, and no one is kept from it either.
