# Source & Need Protocol

*A proposal for the wider coordination layer. The [[ARC Weekly Weave — Design Specification|Weekly Weave]] is the ARC's only live system; everything else in this document is direction, not commitment.*

The coordination layer of the ARC is a protocol and an evolving set of tools. The tools will change.

The protocol beneath them is what this document holds: an ontology small enough to trust, a grammar simple enough to live in, and a handful of invariants that any future tool must honor or not be built.

## Two primes

Everything the coordination layer will ever carry reduces to two moves.

**Source.** Every initiative in the ARC is held by one person who took the vulnerable first step of bringing it into being. The network as a whole has a Source; every offering, gathering, and sourced response has one too. Fields nest inside fields.

Source is acknowledged, never appointed. There are no elections and no co-sources, because creative responsibility does not divide.

**Need & response.** A need is a lack made visible. It is the one thing in the network that arrives without a source, and it waits until someone feels it as theirs and sources its response. *Your need is my allurement.*

The single design constraint that outranks every other: surfacing a need must never feel like exposure.

Whatever tools eventually exist, they will be built from these two primes and nothing else.

## One grammar

The atomic gesture is the **reach**: one person moving toward what another is holding, optionally carrying a word.

> reach → accept → covenant

The one holding accepts or declines, always privately. An accepted reach becomes a covenant: a shared commitment the layer remembers and never enforces. Intimacy generates obligation; the layer holds it and does not police it. Declines carry a word and are never announced.

Nothing is assigned. Nothing is commanded. Everything moves by reach. The layer coordinates; it does not mediate. Where the tools are quiet, the field is quiet.

The grammar particularizes everywhere it touches. Joining the network is itself the handshake: an invitation carrying a word, a first word in return, belonging. Ending is the grammar run in reverse: a covenant completed by mutual acknowledgment, or released.

One grammar, many thresholds.

## The invariants

These hold for every tool the ARC builds or adopts, now and later. A tool that cannot honor them does not belong in the coordination layer, however useful it looks.

**Warm infrastructure.** Every message arrives on behalf of a nameable person or a living covenant. The layer addresses; it does not notify on its own behalf. No "come back," no unread counts, no feeds.

**Designed to be left.** The layer succeeds when members spend less time in it and more time in covenant with one another. Its purpose is to move people into relationship and then recede.

The only measure that matters is hours of real encounter that would not otherwise have happened, and the dignity with which every need is held.

**No scores, no streaks, no simulation of relationship.** No counts of reaches or responses, no leaderboards, no ratings, no likes, no presence indicators, no read receipts. Allurement cannot be scored. Willingness is private. What a machine cannot offer, it must not imitate.

**Thresholds carry weight; dwelling stays light.** The moments that change a relationship deserve ceremony: joining, surfacing a need, reaching, accepting, ending, handing over. One question at a time, given room. Everything in between should be feather-light.

Endings are completions kept in remembrance, never deletions.

**The technology recedes.** The instrumental serves the intrinsic. Any automation that returns attention to encounter may serve the work; any that creates managerial overhead or turns intimate life into data does not.

## What will not be built

Some refusals are as load-bearing as the invariants, and naming them now protects whoever builds later.

No feeds or timelines. No owned messaging: once two people are in covenant, conversation moves to their own channel, deliberately outside the layer. No browsable member directories: practice partners are routed toward each other, not shopped for. No statistics or dashboards shown to members. No public interest counts or popularity signals. Nothing that requires checking a tool to remain in good standing.

If a future feature seems to require one of these, the right move is to surface the conflict, not to build a softened version.

## What is live

One expression of this protocol exists today: the **Weekly Weave**, which routes members into regular dyadic practice over email. It is deliberately the humblest possible form.

A member says they are open. The Weave offers a partner. The two of them take it from there in their own channel. Reach, accept, covenant, recede.

That the first expression is a weekly email is not a compromise. It is the protocol demonstrating that it needs almost nothing to run.

## The horizon

As the network thickens, the field will need more ways to perceive itself. The direction we can see, without committing to any particular form:

- **Offerings and gatherings made visible.** A member moves a gift into the field: a practice space, a reading circle, a gathering with a time and place. Others reach; the one offering accepts; covenants form. Discernment here is clarification, not gatekeeping.
- **Needs made visible.** A place where the network's needs can be surfaced with dignity, held without urgency, and left open until a response is sourced. The [[The Needs & Response System|Needs & Response System]] proposal explores this fully.
- **The field seeing itself.** Over a longer horizon, the same grammar could let needs and offerings move between networks that share the protocol. That is a distant thought, held lightly.

Each of these is a shape, not a plan. Which tools get built, in what order, by whom, and whether some are ever built at all, will be decided in contact with the actual life of the network.

This document exists so that the shape can be seen and felt before anything is committed to. If it becomes the ground for a real conversation with someone who might source the building, it has done its work.

The protocol is settled enough to trust. Everything else is open.
