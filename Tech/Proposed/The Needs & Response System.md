# The Needs & Response System

*A proposal for how the network might sense and respond to its own becoming. The [[ARC Weekly Weave — Design Specification|Weekly Weave]] is the ARC's only live system; everything else here is direction, not commitment.*

> *The cosmos emerges out of need and the response to need.*

The ARC's central practice enacts a pattern: one person's need reaches toward another person's presence, and in the meeting, something real emerges. The need evokes the response. The child's vulnerability calls forth the adult's attunement.

This is not a technique. It is a description of how reality works.

The same pattern operates at the scale of the network. The ARC has needs: roles to be filled, gatherings to be held, members to be accompanied, offerings that want to exist. When these needs are clearly perceived and honestly named, they call forth their own responses from within the field.

Someone steps forward. A capacity emerges. A response is sourced.

The Needs & Response System is the name for making this pattern visible and navigable as the network grows. It is not a task board. It is the network's practice of the responsive cosmos, applied to its own becoming.

## What governs the design

**Need is sacred, not transactional.** A need is not a deficiency to be solved. It is an expression of aliveness reaching toward what it requires. Whatever form the system takes must honor this in language, in structure, in the felt quality of every interaction. A member saying "I need extra support right now" is performing an act of vulnerability, not filing a support ticket, and nothing may flatten that distinction.

**Response is sourcing, not assignment.** When someone responds to a need, they are not completing a task. They are becoming a specific source within the ARC's creative field, taking personal responsibility for a piece of the vision. Their creative authority matters; the quality of their connection to the living field matters.

**The system practices emergence, not engineering.** It surfaces what is alive rather than prescribing what should be. Needs that are planned and needs that arrive unbidden both belong. The ARC builds paths where people are already walking, and holds space for the unexpected.

**The system scales by distributing perception.** Today the Source is the primary sensor of the network's needs. Over time, Practice Holders develop the capacity to sense what the field requires. Eventually, the network itself becomes perceptive. The design must support this progressive distribution without losing coherence.

## The movement

Every need follows a single movement:

> sensing → naming → discernment → sourcing → tending

This is not a workflow. It is the same movement that happens in a dyad — perception, articulation, discernment, response, ongoing presence — operating at the scale of the whole.

**Sensing.** Someone perceives that the field needs something. Sensing is itself a practice; it asks for attunement to the creative field, not just observation of logistics. In the beginning this is mostly the Source. Over time it is Practice Holders and members, differently placed, feeling different parts of the body.

**Naming.** The need is articulated and made visible. This is a vulnerable act, especially for relational needs, and the naming must be honest without being reductive.

"We need someone to steward the network's infrastructure" is a naming. So is "I need a conversation about how to hold authority with integrity." Both are real. Both deserve to be heard.

**Discernment.** What does this need actually require? What kind of source, what closeness to the creative center, what timeline? Discernment is the practice of listening to the need itself — feeling what it is reaching for rather than imposing a solution. Sometimes this is immediate and obvious. Sometimes it asks for conversation.

**Sourcing.** Someone recognizes *this is mine to respond to* and steps forward, or is invited. They become a specific source within the ARC's field.

Sourcing is not volunteering. It is a commitment of personal creative energy, and the sourced response may become a gathering, a role, a project, an act of repair, or something the network has not imagined.

**Tending.** The relationship between need and response stays alive. Is this still serving? Has the need shifted? Does the one sourcing it need support? Tending is what prevents the entropy that turns living responses into dead structures.

## Three kinds of need

**Practical.** *The vessel needs tending.* Infrastructure, roles, coordination, the organizational fabric: a role needs to be filled, a gathering needs to be held, a piece of infrastructure needs to be built. Practical needs tend to be standing or seasonal, and they produce things that persist — systems, tools, documents, roles.

**Relational.** *A person is reaching for presence.* A member needs support through a difficult period, a rupture between two members needs holding, part of the culture has become difficult to feel. Relational needs are almost always emergent, and they route through persons, never through platforms. Posting "I'm struggling" to a board is categorically different from telling a Practice Holder.

The pathway stays simple and human. The member reaches a person — the threshold low enough that "I could use some extra support right now" is enough. A discernment conversation follows: an extra practice session? a particular kind of conversation? a referral beyond the ARC? just to be heard? The response is sourced, directly or by connecting the member with the right person.

And it is tended: a follow-up, "How are you? Did that land?" This is not administration. It is what a loving network does.

Nothing is recorded unless a pattern emerges across many members, suggesting the field itself needs a structural response.

**Creative.** *The creative field is flowering.* Needs born from members' own impulses: the desire to offer something the network does not yet have. A contemplative reading circle, a local in-person gathering, a body-practice session, a small group exploring a lineage together. Creative needs are the sign of a healthy field. They emerge bottom-up, ask for the least oversight, and want only visibility and lightweight structure — never over-formalization of what wants to stay organic.

## Timescales

Needs live on different clocks, and the system must hold each without forcing it into the shape of another.

**Standing needs** persist as long as the network does at a given size, produce ongoing roles and relationships, and deserve re-discernment at each growth threshold: what the ARC needed at 150 may not be what it needs at 300.

**Seasonal needs** are tied to a moment or transition — a cohort arriving, a gathering being prepared. They have natural beginnings and endings, and the endings ask for graceful completion: not just "done," but "what did we learn, and what did this become?"

**Emergent needs** arise from practice and dissolve when met. They cannot be anticipated. The field needs enough porosity to receive what it could not have planned for, without demanding that it be pre-categorized.

## The intimacy gradient

Not every need can be met by just anyone. Part of discernment is feeling how much relational proximity to the ARC's creative center a response requires.

Some responses shape the container itself — they touch the network's identity, its commitments, its public face. Whoever sources them needs deep, ongoing conversation with the Source; they need to feel the ARC from the inside, not just understand it conceptually. Call these **source-intimate**.

Other responses ask for developed practice capacity and real familiarity with the commitments, without the Source's ongoing involvement: **holder-proximate**.

And most ask for nothing beyond membership and goodwill: **member-open**. The reading circles, the local gatherings, the collaborations — the field is open, and the network's role is visibility, not gatekeeping.

The gradient describes what the response requires, never what a person is worth. The ARC's design language asks for more intimacy with the Source than a book club does. Neither is more important. They require different things.

And the gradient must be held next to a danger the safeguarding work names plainly: in the founding phase, proximity to the Source is both the access gradient and the classic manipulation vector. A gradient of proximity can quietly become a ladder of standing, and a ladder of standing is the cultic pattern wearing design language.

Two disciplines keep it honest. The gradient exists to protect tender responses — so that what touches the network's identity is held in enough relationship to be held well — not to confer rank. And its direction of travel is outward: as the field matures, what was source-intimate becomes holder-proximate, and what was holder-proximate becomes member-open.

A gradient that only ever steepens has failed.

## Diffuse coordination

At larger scales, coordination is usually organized through command and assignment: someone at the center decides what needs to happen and directs others to carry it out. The ARC is exploring another possibility, which we call **diffuse coordination**. Needs become perceptible across the network, and responses are sourced by people who feel intrinsically moved to respond to them.

Two clarifications keep this honest.

Visibility does not make every need everyone's responsibility. A member who practices faithfully and sources nothing has already made a complete contribution. The point of making needs visible is not to distribute obligation. It is to let allurement find the people it belongs to.

And an unmet need is not evidence of insufficient faith, clarity, or participation. Some needs wait a long season for their response. Some dissolve unanswered. A need that stays open is information about the field, not a failure of the field, and the system must be able to hold an open need without generating urgency, guilt, or shame around it.

## The Needs Garden

We carry an image for where this could go. Call it the **Needs Garden**. The metaphor is deliberate: needs are planted, tended, and harvested. Some bear fruit quickly. Some take a season. Some are perennials.

In the Garden, a need is not a task description. It is a clear, honest naming, written in first person by whoever sensed it: "We need a design language that communicates the ARC's character across everything it touches." Or: "I'm looking for a conversation partner around integrating this practice with my Christian contemplative background."

Each need carries its own story — what prompted it, what discernment has happened, what the response is becoming. A living narrative, not a status log. A need is eventually released: met, transformed, or honestly let go.

What would live there: practical needs, creative offerings that want visibility across the network, the seasonal and standing needs that define the network's ongoing shape. What would not: relational needs, which route through persons; and needs met immediately through conversation, because not everything needs to be recorded.

Conversation first, platform second. If a need can be met by a phone call, it does not need an entry anywhere. The system serves the practice; the practice does not serve the system.

The Garden's refusals matter as much as its features. Needs are sensed privately and clarified in relationship before they are published, so that surfacing never feels like exposure. Anonymity while a need is held; recognition follows fruit.

No counts of interest, ever. A need that has waited months looks no different from one planted yesterday, because staleness signals are shame architecture.

The Garden is an image, not a build. A version of it was once prototyped and set aside; what remains is the direction. Whether it becomes a shared document, a simple page, or something not yet imagined will be decided in contact with the actual life of the network, in the grammar the [[Source & Need Protocol]] describes.

It is possible that a future tool, including an intelligent one, could help notice patterns across many needs — three Practice Holders independently sensing the same strain, say, suggesting a structural need no one of them could see alone.

If such a tool ever exists, it would be an instrument in service of human sensing, never a sensor with standing of its own, and it must never simulate the attunement that relational needs require.

## The language is load-bearing

Whatever form this takes, the words are not ornamentation. A need is not a ticket, an issue, or a task. A response is not an assignment or a deliverable. The one who responds is not an owner or a lead; they are the source of that response.

Sensing, naming, discernment, sourcing, tending, offering, releasing: this vocabulary keeps the system honest about what it is holding. The language is the operating system. Warm, honest, unhurried, non-instrumental — and any interface, human or technical, that cannot carry that tone does not belong here.

## Horizon questions

These are the live edges, held as questions rather than resolved. They are invitations, not failures.

- How is the capacity to sense needs cultivated beyond the Source? What practices help Practice Holders develop it, and how is a personal preference distinguished from a genuine field-level need?
- How do relational needs stay held at larger scales without bureaucratizing vulnerability? At 150 members a handful of Practice Holders suffices; what holds at ten times that, and what must never change?
- How much curation do creative offerings need? When does an offering diverge enough from the commitments that discernment is called for, and who holds it?
- What happens when discernment misreads a need's gradient — when something that seemed member-open turns out to touch the container? Is there a graceful way to re-discern?
- If a member's creative impulse outgrows what the ARC can hold — if their need is really a calling to source something new — how does the network support a graceful fork?
- When a sourced response grows into sustained work, how is the person carrying it sustained, without turning gift into wage?

## Who senses

Today the Source is the primary sensor of the network's needs. That is appropriate to this moment and is not the destination.

As Practice Holders form, they become co-sensors, noticing across their encounters what the field requires. In time, the network itself becomes perceptive: many people, differently placed, feeling different parts of the whole.

The system scales by distributing perception, not by centralizing control.

What we are practicing toward is a network that can be honest about what it needs and trust that the response will come. That transparency would itself be a teaching — a demonstration that a body can organize around need and response rather than command and control.
