# The Practice Holder Formation Pathway
## What Practice Holders Are Formed In and How They Are Recognized

> Practice Holders are the load-bearing role that makes growth without dilution possible. The conditions for their formation are therefore also the conditions for the ARC's capacity to meet the world's need for attuned loving presence. This document describes how that formation happens, how it is recognized, and how it is held in integrity.

---

## 1. Orientation

### What This Document Is

This is the ARC's articulation of how it understands and approaches the formation of Practice Holders — the people whose practice has matured to the point that they can hold the dyadic field for others, source learning spaces, and contribute to the formation of those coming behind them.

It is written at the moment when the ARC is forming its first Practice Holders, when the question of formation has moved from abstract to concrete, and when collaborators are beginning to participate in the design of the formation infrastructure itself.

It is not a credentialing document. It is an invitation: to those already capacitated as holders to step into the language and relationships of the ARC, and to those forming toward the role to find a clear shape for what they are forming toward.

### Who It Is For

This document is for prospective and current Practice Holders, for ARC members thinking about whether the role might be theirs, for collaborators helping design the formation infrastructure, and for the Source himself — who must remain accountable to the shape of the pathway as it evolves and to the principle that credentialing authority is meant to diffuse outward from him over time.

### The Central Thesis

The ARC's purpose is to meet, at scale, the world's need for attuned loving presence. Practice Holders are the load-bearing role that makes growth without dilution possible. Everything in this document follows from this fact.

The thesis of this pathway is that holder formation can be neither fully spontaneous nor fully prescribed. It must be supported by infrastructure — learning spaces, structures of self-discernment, relational accountability, formal recognition — while remaining grounded in the recognition that what makes a holder is *presence itself*, not the methods through which presence is scaffolded. The pathway describes the support without enclosing the formation, and remains explicitly generative: instances of learning space and recognition are expected to emerge through network practice rather than be specified in advance.

### Position in the Validation Landscape

Holder formation is one of three levels at which the ARC validates practice capacity, named together in *Capacity Validation Infrastructure* (internal working material, not a corpus companion). The other two are entry validation, which lives in the ARC Entryway — the free relational container of orientation, relationship, and mutual discernment through which everyone enters — and network field capacity, a live design area not yet drafted.

The three levels share an epistemology — practice capacity discloses itself through participation, validators must themselves be practitioners, assessment and formation are intertwined, recognition is living rather than permanent — and serve different decisions at different thresholds. Holder validation is the highest-fidelity individual-level validation work the ARC does. It rests on entry validation having been done well (so that the field of those forming toward holder maturity is itself capacitated) and feeds into network field capacity (so that the supply of holders becomes a property of the network the network can sense in itself).

This document focuses on the holder level. Cross-references point to the others where relevant.

## 2. What a Practice Holder Is

Practice Holders are practitioners the network recognizes as reliable holders of the practice: people whose capacity for presence has matured to the point that they can reliably hold dyadic unfolding for others and help sustain the quality of practice across the network.

The phrase *Practice Holder* does double work. It names a role — what the person does in the network — and a developmental stage — where the person is in their own practice. In the ARC's logic, these collapse into one. To hold the practice is to be at a certain place in one's own practice; the role is downstream of the formation.

What the person holds is not material to be processed. It is the conditions under which presence can show up between two people and reality can unfold accordingly. A holder is someone whose orientation toward intrinsic value — toward truth, toward love, toward the real — is sincere enough that their presence creates the conditions for another's presence to extend.

The holder is another practitioner — equal, mortal, doing the same practice — whose maturation in that practice qualifies them to hold space for others. The role exists to hold the practice and sustain its quality; its authority begins and ends in the practice container. This boundedness is load-bearing: it is the structural feature that keeps the holder pathway from becoming a credentialing system that produces derivative authorities. (See *Anti-Cult Design Patterns* for elaboration of this risk and its design response.)

It is also significant that many people in and around the ARC may already be capacitated as holders. They may simply not yet have the language game by which the ARC names the role. Part of what this document offers is that language — for those who can find their own practice in it.

## 3. The Formation Pathway

Formation happens through sustained practice, apprenticeship, real holding responsibility, and demonstrated capacity. The ARC is now forming its first Practice Holders. The elements below describe how those four grounds take shape in the network's current life.

### Entry into Formation

Entry into holder formation is currently emergent. People express interest. They are met in conversation by the Source or by existing holders. They find their way into the practice and into the language. There is no application form, no curriculum-to-complete, no designated cohort.

This is intentional in the early phase. The ARC is small enough that emergent entry can be held relationally; the conditions for more formal entry will likely arise as the network grows. For now, the relevant move for someone drawn to the role is to reach out — to begin a conversation.

(Note: entry into holder formation is distinct from entry into the ARC itself. Membership in the ARC comes first, through the ARC Entryway. Holder formation is a movement *within* membership, not a parallel onboarding.)

### Foundations Training

Foundations Training (whose first cohort ran as the Unfolding Circle) is one expression of the formation pathway, although not the only one. Many of those forming toward holder maturity will move through the training. Some will already be capacitated and will use the training to learn the ARC's language and practice forms. Others will form into holder-readiness through the training itself.

The training is not a credentialing pathway. Completion of the training does not, by itself, confer holder recognition. What the training does is offer a sustained container in which the practice can be developed and the language game can be inhabited.

### Apprenticeship and Real Holding Responsibility

Formation deepens through walking with those who already hold. A forming holder practices alongside more mature holders, receives their attention on the texture of their holding, and gradually takes on real responsibility: holding dyads where greater capacity is needed, helping receive newer practitioners, supporting spaces within the training and the network's rhythm. Capacity is demonstrated in actual holding, witnessed by those who can recognize it — not asserted, and not inferred from completion of anything.

Apprenticeship here is relational, not hierarchical. The one apprenticed to is a peer further along in the same practice, and the relationship is itself held inside the network's agreements and safeguarding commitments. What the apprenticeship transmits is texture: the lived feel of holding well, which no document can carry.

### Learning Spaces

Beyond the training, the ARC supports formation through *learning spaces* — bounded contexts in which one dimension of practice is held with deliberate constraint. Learning spaces are distinct from practice spaces. A practice space holds the full unfolding of dyadic encounter. A learning space narrows attention, often along a single way of looking, until that way of looking becomes fluent and available in the wider practice.

Examples of learning spaces (each currently in various stages of design and discovery):

- A *parts space* — practice constrained toward attending to parts and the relations among them
- A *somatic space* — practice constrained toward bodily awareness as the primary field
- A *beholding space* — practice constrained toward seeing and being seen as presence
- An *inquiry space* — practice constrained toward question and clean language

Each learning space is essentially a way of looking inhabited as practice. The way of looking is a frame; the learning space is what one does to inhabit the frame somatically and relationally so that the way of looking becomes fluent in the fuller dyadic field.

Learning spaces are not arranged in a typical sequence. They are a network of optional deepenings, sourced as people are called to them.

The criterion for any candidate learning space is whether its constraint reliably forces a capacitation that *extends the territories in which presence can be the response*. Constraints that work only by technique — that can be satisfied without presence — are not generative; they are method-traps.

### Structures of Self-Discernment

Self-discernment about where one is in formation, where one is lagging, and where one is called to deepen is itself a practice that needs support. The ARC does not yet have these structures fully built. They are an active design area and will likely include peer reflection forms, supervised practice contexts, and dialogues with more senior holders. Members forming toward holder readiness are invited into the design of these structures, not just their consumption.

### Recognition Comes Last

Within the pathway, formal recognition (validation, see Section 5) is the last move, not the first. People form into holder maturity through practice; recognition follows. The reverse — being recognized first and forming into the role afterward — is a known failure mode, and one of the conditions under which the cultic pattern hardens around the credential rather than being addressed by the practice.

## 4. What Practice Holders Are Resourced With

A Practice Holder, once recognized, is resourced with:

- *A central place in the Weekly Weave.* Holders are woven into regular dyadic practice with members across the network, both because they hold the practice well and because doing so capacitates those they are paired with. (The live Weave is simple. Weighting holders more heavily and across more diverse pairings is a future direction for the Weave's design, not a present mechanism.)
- *Sourcing privileges for spaces in the ARC.* Holders can propose and host spaces — learning spaces, gatherings, focused practice containers — within the ARC's broader infrastructure.
- *Invitation to support trainings and offerings.* Holders are part of how the ARC scales its educational and formative offerings, including Foundations Training itself.
- *Participation in the validation of others.* Holders are part of the recognition process for those forming behind them. (See Section 5.) They are also expected, over time, to take on entry-conversation work in the ARC Entryway as part of the diffusion of discernment authority.
- *Mutual support among holders.* Holders form a connective tissue across the network — peers in practice, peers in formation, mutual sources of feedback, accountability, and care.

There is no required ongoing commitment beyond the practice itself. Holders contribute as they are called and able. The aspiration is that holders remain available in the Weekly Weave as a way of supporting members tasting the practice, but availability is offered freely, not enforced.

One boundary is absolute. They do not pursue or accept sexual or romantic involvement with members in their care. *This is a hard line.* (See *Anti-Cult Design Patterns*, Section 4.)

## 5. Validation

The validation system — by which someone is recognized as a Practice Holder — is the most live design question in this pathway. Formation itself is settled ground: sustained practice, apprenticeship, real holding responsibility, demonstrated capacity. How the network *marks* that a formation has ripened — the topology of recognition — is still being discovered, and the first recognitions will teach us most of what we need to know.

The shared epistemology underneath all candidate validation forms is articulated in *Capacity Validation Infrastructure*: validators must themselves be practitioners; validation cannot be fully externalized into checklists; assessment and formation are intertwined; recognition is living, not permanent.

### Current Working Hypothesis: Peer Accumulation

In the working hypothesis, validations accumulate from already-validated holders, beginning with the Source. A candidate seeking recognition meets with one or more existing holders, who attest (or do not) that they see in the candidate the maturation of practice the role requires. With enough attestations from across the network, the candidate is recognized as a holder.

Because formation runs through apprenticeship, the holders a candidate has walked with will usually be among those attesting — they are the ones who have witnessed the holding directly. The design question peer accumulation answers is how to keep that witness from collapsing into a single sponsor's judgment: attestations must come from across the network, so that recognition belongs to the field rather than to any one relationship.

This hypothesis has the structural property of diffusing credentialing authority outward from the Source over time, as more holders form and the validating field thickens. The Source's weight as initial validator is high in the early phase; it diminishes, intentionally, as the field matures. This diffusion is itself a design commitment.

The threshold (number of validations, topology of the validating circle) is not yet specified. It will be discovered through practice rather than fixed in advance.

### Other Forms Being Held

Other recognition forms are being considered, and the actual system may combine elements:

- *Recursive sourcing.* A candidate is recognized when they have successfully sourced or held practice spaces and others present feel met. Validation comes from the people they hold for. Strength: validates capacity from below. Risk: subject to satisfaction dynamics rather than presence-quality.
- *Embedded recognition.* No discrete validation moment; recognition is woven into ongoing practice rhythms — moments where existing holders explicitly name what they see in someone's practice. Validation as ongoing texture rather than event. Strength: anti-credentialing by design. Risk: too informal to function as a marker.

### Principles for Whatever the System Becomes

Whatever the actual validation form, several principles are load-bearing:

- *Peer-grounded.* Recognition comes from the practice field, not from a centralized authority.
- *Diffusing.* The Source's weight in validation diminishes over time; the path toward distributed credentialing authority is real and visible, not aspirational.
- *Textured.* Validations are attestations of what was specifically seen in someone's practice — not a binary credential. Texture preserves relational quality and resists gaming.
- *Living.* Attestations are best understood as what was seen at the time, not permanent credentials.
- *Cross-pollinating.* Recognition probably wants to require attestations from multiple parts of the network, not just one's local circle.

### What the System Does Not Do

The validation system does not certify spiritual attainment, ethical perfection, or any quality beyond what holding the practice requires. It does not produce a permanent caste. It is recognition, not elevation.

## 6. Drift and De-recognition

Holders can drift. People who once held the practice well can lose it — through life circumstances, through interior shifts, through the slow corrosion that comes from being recipient of any kind of recognition over time. The validation system must therefore include pathways for de-recognition as well as for recognition.

The general shape: members must be able to surface concerns about a holder without social or material cost. Concerns can include behavior in dyads, drift toward junior-Source dynamics, instrumentalizing orientation under cover of practice form, breach of the hard lines (sexual involvement with members in care, financial exploitation), or the general sense — felt before it can be named — that something is off.

Specific structures for receiving these concerns are not yet built. They will be developed in concert with the broader awareness and safeguarding infrastructure described in *Anti-Cult Design Patterns* (Section 6, Layer III). Until those structures exist, the working pathway is direct contact with the Source or with existing holders trusted by the member raising the concern.

The principle governing de-recognition: a holder whose practice has drifted is not a person who has failed; they are a person whose practice needs to be met by the network. Sometimes the meeting includes stepping back from the holder role for a season. Sometimes it includes stepping aside permanently. Sometimes it includes deeper accountability work alongside continued practice. The aim is not punitive but restorative — to the practice, to the field, and to the person involved.

When holders disagree about a candidate's readiness, about practice direction, or about each other, the design question becomes how disagreement is held. This is an open thread. The current working orientation is that disagreement is itself a practice — to be entered with sincere orientation toward what is real, not with the orientation of winning. The structures that will support this practice over time are being discovered.

## 7. Open Threads

This pathway is generative, not prescriptive. The following are explicitly open and are invitations to participate in the design:

- **The validation system.** Formation is settled; the topology of recognition is not. The working hypothesis is described; the working form will be discovered as the first holders are recognized. This is the most active design question in the pathway.
- **De-recognition mechanisms.** The principle is named; the structure is not yet built.
- **Disagreement between holders.** The orientation is named; the practical forms are open.
- **The relationship to external trainings.** Aletheia, Internal Family Systems, Focusing, somatic lineages — how do these relate to formation in the ARC? The ARC Entryway receives people whose grounding comes through them; their relation to holder formation specifically is a further question.
- **The Source's evolving role.** As authority diffuses outward, what does the Source's role become? Founding source, senior holder, something else? This evolution is committed-to in principle but not yet specified.
- **Compensation.** Holders are currently uncompensated. As the network sustains itself financially, the question of how holders are economically supported will arise. This is a downstream design question.
- **Specific learning spaces.** Each candidate learning space is itself a design question — what its constraint is, what capacitation it forces, how it is sourced.
- **Holder formation as feedback into the practice.** If validation is also formative, what's the loop by which the texture of attestations refines the practice the network is developing? This is the most theoretically interesting open thread.

Members and collaborators are invited into all of these threads.

## 8. Design Commitments

These are the load-bearing commitments of the holder formation pathway:

- *Distributed authority.* The credentialing authority of the Source is meant to diffuse outward over time. The pathway is structurally oriented toward this diffusion; the Source carries responsibility for the diffusion being real.
- *Generative not prescriptive.* The pathway describes principles and current working forms; instances of learning space and recognition are expected to emerge through network practice.
- *Presence-as-response, not method-as-credential.* What makes a holder is presence, not method. Methods scaffold presence; they do not substitute for it. The pathway is designed to consistently dissolve method into presence rather than calcify method into credential.
- *Bounded to the practice.* The role exists to hold the practice; its authority begins and ends there. This boundedness is structurally as load-bearing as the role's content. (See *Anti-Cult Design Patterns* for the failure modes this commitment is designed to meet.)
- *Recursive with the practice.* The system that recognizes holders is the same practice the recognition certifies. Mutual recognition of presence is what dyadic practice is; validation is that recognition made explicit and durable.
- *Living.* This document is a starting condition, not a final form. It will be revised as the ARC encounters its actual life.

## 9. The Limits of This Document

This document is not a guarantee. It cannot prevent what it describes from going wrong. It is a clarification of orientation, written at the moment when the first Practice Holders are forming, and when the structures that will hold this work over time are still being discovered.

Every documented intention can fail. Every pathway can drift. Every form of validation can be hollowed out from within. This document does not prevent that. What it does is make the commitments visible enough that drift is recognizable, name the live questions so they can be addressed, and create a reference point that members and collaborators can return to as the pathway evolves.

The deeper truth is that the ARC's holder formation is finally not a system. It is a practice. It is people forming into presence-as-response and recognizing each other as they do so. The document is one part of that work.
