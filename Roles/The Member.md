# The Member

*The Member Agreement holds the covenant. This document describes what membership is and what it asks.*

## What membership is

Membership is a covenant of practice, relationship, and shared responsibility for the life and integrity of the network. A person enters it through the ARC Entryway, on a mutual yes, by signing the Member Agreement.

Membership is not purchased. The practice and relational life of the network are freely available to members, and entry, access, standing, and renewal are never conditioned on financial contribution. A member is not a customer, a user, or a subscriber. A member is a practitioner the network has received, and who has received the network.

## The work

**Practice.** The first and sufficient form of participation. A member shows up to dyads and Gatherings, offers presence, receives presence, honors the agreements, and contributes to the relational life of the network. The Weekly Weave routes members into dyadic unfolding week by week; the Gathering renews each member's relationship with the whole.

A member does not need to lead, build, or source a project in order to belong. Faithful participation in the practice is already a complete contribution.

**Truthfulness in relationship.** The commitments of the Member Agreement — good faith, honesty, responsibility for one's own projections, feedback offered and received in relationship, confidentiality, the safeguarding lines — are not administrative conditions. They are the covenant through which the field stays trustworthy enough to practice in.

**Surfacing what is felt.** When something feels off — about the field, about another member, about the Source — the member brings it forward rather than carrying it alone. The network's capacity to perceive itself lives in its members' willingness to speak.

## What membership may grow into

As members become more deeply connected to the people, purpose, and practice of the ARC, they may begin to feel more of what is happening in the network — where something is needed, where something wants protecting, where a new possibility is trying to take form. Sometimes a member encounters a need and recognizes: *this is mine to respond to.* The ARC calls that sourcing, and any member may become the source of a particular response they bring into being — a gathering, a role, a project, an act of repair. None of this is required. All of it is welcome.

## Standing and leaving

Participation in the whole should increase each member's agency: a healthy network makes its members more able to sense, choose, create, dissent, refuse, and leave. Departure is honored at any time, as a real outcome — not a failure, and never held against anyone. Giving has no bearing on membership, access, standing, relationships, or voice. The right of exit is part of the safeguarding architecture, and it is absolute.
